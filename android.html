Android Widget

A home-screen widget for your phone

What it is

A small Android app whose only job is to put Happening Now on your home screen: the time, your weather, headlines for each of your news categories, and your stock watchlist. It comes in three sizes, from a full page down to a 2×2 clock-and-weather square. Tapping anything opens the site.

No ads, no account and no tracking. It shows the same free-to-read sources as the site.

Download the widget app

Android 9 or newer. About 100 KB. There is no iPhone version: iOS does not allow apps to be installed this way.

Installing it

The app comes straight from this site, not from the Play Store, so Android asks a couple of extra questions the first time. Open this page in Chrome on your phone, then:

  1. Tap Download the widget app. If Chrome warns about the file type, choose Download anyway.
  2. Open the downloaded file. Android will ask whether Chrome may install apps: tap Settings, switch on Allow from this source, and go back.
  3. Tap Install, then Open.
  4. Choose what the widget shows. Set up here lets you pick your town, news topics and watchlist in the app. If you already use this site on your phone, Use my happening-now.net settings brings them over instead: the Today page opens with a Send to phone widget button.
  5. Under Add a widget, pick a size.

Bringing settings over from the site works when you use it in Chrome, or as the installed Happening Now app: that is where your location, news categories and watchlist are kept.

Keeping it in step with the site

This applies if you brought your settings over from the site. The widget cannot read the site's settings by itself. After the first send, whenever you change your location, topics or watchlist on this phone, the site shows an Update phone widget bar. One tap brings the widget up to date.

Updates

Because it is not from a store, the app cannot update itself. Once a day it checks this site for a newer version and, when there is one, says so on the widget. Come back to this page and download again; installing over the old copy keeps your settings.

Privacy

The app has no account, no ads and no analytics. It asks for one permission: internet access. Settings you choose in the app stay on your phone; settings brought from the site travel inside a link on your own phone. Neither is uploaded. Finding your town sends the ZIP code or name you type to a lookup service (Zippopotam or Open-Meteo), as the site does. To draw the widget it fetches the same things the site does: your forecast from Open-Meteo using your saved location, and headlines and stock quotes through this site's own server. The privacy policy has the details.

Good to know

Android only lets home-screen widgets refresh about every half hour; the refresh arrow on the widget fetches at once. Widgets cannot detect swipes, so you move between news categories by tapping a name or an arrow. The app is open source, in the same repository as this site.
